# Relaxation range adjustment across temperatures

Implemented on 2026-09-19; hardware validation is pending. Set `adjust_range: true` on `exp.t2` or `exp.t1` to use measured-tail decisions. Its default is false. [The T1/T2 temperature series](../../protocols/temperature_series_t1t2.yaml) and [T2-only series](../../protocols/temperature_series_t2.yaml) show `temp.set` and `temp.wait` inside `foreach`. Edit the sample, 3318 G field, temperature values, and shipped preset choices for the actual sample and spectrometer before live use.

For one T2 measurement without a temperature loop, use [t2_auto_range.yaml](../../protocols/t2_auto_range.yaml). Its automatic window is the measured 2τ range; it keeps the preset echo-integration window.

The protocol's `tau_start`, `tau_step`, `points`, `t_start` and `t_end` seed the first measurement of each kind. Range assessment starts after the first full scan, before stopping or projecting the scan count from `target_snr`. A confirmed plateau keeps the same acquisition and its accumulated data; normal SNR control then continues. A clearly unfinished tail can trigger one early extension, so the runner does not first spend the full SNR budget on a range already known to be too short.

Every final measured curve with a confirmed plateau is accepted as the current curve, even when it has more baseline points than the preferred future coverage. After the hard `relaxation_fit` judge passes, the accepted curve recommends a range for the next temperature in the same session: about 55% baseline points for T2 or 47 plateau points for T1. A confirmed but short plateau does not trigger a repeat of the current measurement.

The recommendation carries only range settings, not the old integration window, receiver phase, repetition rate, fit, or data. Warming may shorten the next sampled span by at most 25%; cooling or unknown temperature cannot shorten the latest measured span. Extension remains possible. The recommendation is held only for the current protocol run, keyed by sample, T1/T2 kind, field, preset, actual calibrated pulses and seed controls. Temperature is deliberately excluded. An unmatched context starts from the protocol seed. The cache is cleared when an RV move invalidates fine calibration. When temperature state exists, `temp.wait` must have confirmed arrival before a recommendation is used. A dry-run, failed fit, skipped step, or Stop cannot update it.

## Operator calibration

The July oTerPhenyl curves supplied the visual reference for range selection. The operator accepted the full 80 K T2 curve (about 22.765 µs) and the full 80 K T1 curve (about 1.648 ms), with additional T1 time still useful. At 240 K, the selected endpoints were 12–14 µs for T2 and 700–800 µs for T1. The implemented future-range targets are therefore measured-tail rules, not fixed endpoints at those temperatures: 50–60% T2 baseline (target 55%) and 45–50 T1 plateau points (target 47).

## Measured-tail rule

The complex trace is rotated using the existing phase convention. The reference is the last fifth of the T2 data, capped at 100 points, or the last 30 T1 points. The allowed deviation is 1% of the measured contrast between the median of the first five points and the reference mean. The reference's first and last thirds must agree within that tolerance including a noise margin. Earlier block means are checked against the reference with a Student-t noise margin; at least one qualifying block must precede the reference itself. This is an operational measured-plateau check, not a simultaneous confidence bound or fitted-asymptote test.

Fewer than 60 points, invalid data, or insufficient measured contrast cannot support a range decision. The early check uses the complete accumulated curve after each full scan, including the final ADC point. If noise makes the decision uncertain, it can examine up to three completed scans, or fewer when the scan ceiling is lower. SNR stopping and projection wait until the range decision is resolved or this short assessment ends; duration and scan limits remain active throughout. An uncertain decision after these scans keeps the current range and resumes normal SNR control. There is no late repeat after the full accumulation, even if the final curve still lacks a confirmed plateau.

A visibly unfinished but usable tail with significant drift can receive one extension of about twice the sampled span. The runner checks the revised sequence, point ceiling and remaining `max_duration` budget before stopping the current acquisition at the completed scan. If an extension cannot be used, the current acquisition continues with normal SNR control. If it can, the early acquisition is saved and one new acquisition runs over the extended range with the requested SNR and remaining time budget. Both acquisition files are retained; their data are not combined across different grids. The revised acquisition cannot trigger another repair. Mere noise does not justify an extension.

## Acquisitions and timing

T2 preserves its pulse timing and grid step where possible. T1 preserves logarithmic density where possible and counts the actual snapped, deduplicated grid. `adjust_max_points` defaults to 4096 and caps automatically resized sweeps; an unchanged range may keep the protocol's larger point count. The `range_adjustment` result records the early check, measured coverage, the reason for a repeat or reuse, and both file paths when two acquisitions ran. Top-level `start_s`/`end_s` describe the saved axis; T1's `t_start`/`t_end` are logarithmic-grid controls.

The temperature examples combine `adjust_range: true`, `target_snr: 20`, `scans: 64` and `max_duration: 600 s`. The 64 scans are a ceiling for each acquisition, not a mandatory accumulation. A suitable range retains its first scans while accumulating toward SNR 20. When an early extension is needed, only the first one to three scans use the original range; the extended acquisition then applies normal SNR control. Scan/time limits and an optimistic SNR projection can leave the final SNR below the target.

The first seeded T1 acquisition uses the protocol's explicit, automatic, or preset repetition rate on tunable sources; Nd:YAG uses its fixed 9.9 Hz rate and does not support automatic rate tuning. A T1 acquisition with a carried or repaired range recalculates the maximum timing-compatible rate in 0.1 Hz steps using the complete Log Time worker/driver preflight across all points and pulse timing. This rate is a sequence limit, not proof that the sample has physically recovered between shots. The range cache never carries an old `tune.rep_rate` recommendation. T2 retains its chosen rate; on tunable sources, the T2-only example measures `tune.rep_rate` after each temperature move before using `rep_rate: auto`. This is the continuous live procedure: three fresh echo curves within 5% at each rate, fixed τ and field, one open card for the grid. Its integration window is separate from this document's T1/T2 acquisition-range adjustment.

`max_duration` covers analysis, preflights and both acquisitions; it is a projected budget rather than a hard interrupt. A repeat is skipped if one revised scan cannot fit. The budget is checked before stopping the original acquisition; once it has stopped, the extension always runs at least one scan, so the overrun is limited to saving the early data. An unexpected error in the early check is reported as `failed` and normal SNR control resumes. Stop propagates normally. An explicit protocol retry starts a new step attempt, each with at most one early extension.

## Offline checks

Run the scripts `early_range_checks.py`, `scan_boundary_checks.py`, `relaxation_range_checks.py` and `relaxation_timing_checks.py` from `atomize/script_examples/epr_auto/` with `python3 <path> test`. They cover early decisions and SNR control, complete-scan synchronization, range carryover and sequence timing. The two temperature protocols pass `validate` and `run --test`; dry-run data are canned, so these runs do not prove measured range carryover. The GUI/engine equivalence harness covers worker-argument parity. Live temperature-series validation remains open.
